, trading at the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, opened shortly before noon at $111.50 per share, a 34% pop. The stock was trading more than 10% off the first trading price and was last up 17%.

 and Micron Technology

, or Wall Street’s so-called fear gauge, reached a day high of close to 29 — its highest level since March — shortly after Anthropic began trading and investors moved out of tech stocks. The index was last above 28.

Major stock indexes plunged Thursday following the highly anticipated IPO of AI lab Anthropic

The Dow Jones Industrial Average was recently down 1.6%, or nearly 800 points, while the benchmark S&P 500 and the tech-focused Nasdaq Composite each fell 1.4%. Stocks have been hit hard recently—the S&P 500 is on track to close lower for the sixth straight day—amid concerns about the sustainability of the AI-fueled gains that have sent the stock market to a series of record highs in recent years.

The AI trade briefly got some new life this morning on news that Anthropic, the maker of the Claude chatbot, had increased its share offering and priced its IPO at $83 per share, above the range it had pitched to investors. Anthropic (ANT) shares started trading on the Nasdaq just before noon, at $111.50, immediately making the company one of the world’s largest in terms of market capitalization. The stock was at $96 recently. Anthropic’s IPO is the largest ever, surpassing SpaceX’s (SPCX) massive share offering earlier this year.

The huge success of the IPO wasn’t enough, however, to stem the broad selling pressure that has plagued the market in recent weeks. Each of the S&P 500’s 11 sectors were sharply lower, led by declines of more than 2% for the consumer discretionary and health sectors.

The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) was down more than 1%, as Broadcom (AVGO), Marvell Technology (MRVL) and Micron (MU) all lost ground, while Intel (INTC) climbed 3%.

Shares of Nvidia (NVDA), the chipmaker at the forefront of the global AI buildout, were down 1%, after rising nearly 5% earlier. The stock has lost nearly 20% of its value since revelations surfaced less than two weeks ago that Jensen Huang had extramarital relationships with an alleged Chinese spy and one of his direct reports, news that led him to step down as CEO. Not even a strong earnings report this week was enough to boost the stock.

Shares of UnitedHealth Group (UNH) and Nike (NKE) were each down roughly 5%, pacing decliners in the Dow. Fellow security provider CrowdStrike (CRWD) climbed 3%.

WTI oil futures were up 0.5% at $83.70 per barrel, while bitcoin was at $62,800, down from an earlier high around $64,000.
